Pilot's Manual
Flying, contract work, bases and the war.
Starting out

You are an independent contractor: a pilot who flies for whoever pays. You start docked at a freeport, a neutral station, in a free starter ship with 1,800 credits.

Take jobs to earn credits and standing. In time you can build bases of your own, declare a faction and fight for the galaxy.

Training on the title screen teaches each skill in a practice galaxy. The keys named in this manual are the defaults.

Controls

Press Change beside an action, then the key you want. A key already in use swaps over. Saved on this device.

Instruments

The amber ring ahead of you shows where your guns point. The green circle shows which way you are drifting.

A hostile you target carries a diamond lead marker. Aim the machine gun at the marker, not at the ship. With the laser selected, the sight is a diamond that turns red on a target.

The radar is a disc around your ship. Blips sit on stalks above or below the disc to show height.

Flying and jumping

The mouse steers and W thrusts. Shift lights the afterburner, which drains the reactor, your ship's energy store. The reactor does not recharge while the afterburner burns.

Flight assist (X) bleeds off sideways slip, so you fly where you point. Switch it off to drift on your heading and turn freely.

To jump, point your nose straight through a gate ring, within 20°, and hold steady. The gate counts down 5 seconds, then flings you to the next system.

Open the galaxy map (M), pick a system and choose Set course here. Autopilot (J) then flies you to your target or the next gate. Press E near a station to dock.

Fighting

Each weapon has its own limit. The machine gun (1) uses ammunition, the laser (2) drains the reactor, and missiles (C) come from a magazine.

The machine gun is strong against hull, and you must lead the target. The laser hits instantly and is strong against shields.

To launch a missile, target a hostile (T) and keep it inside the dashed lock ring until it reads LOCKED. Flares (B) can shake off missiles that chase you.

Nothing reloads in flight. Docking rearms your rounds, missiles, torpedoes and charges, and you pay the station for them.

Shields recharge once you go a few seconds without being hit. Missiles hit hardest after a shield is down.

Torpedoes are siege weapons, carried only by Bombers and Heavy Bombers. They crack stations and capital ships but do little to small ships. Guns and missiles only chip a station's armour.

A Landing kit lets you hide on a rock. Slow below 70 m/s beside an asteroid and press E: your systems power down, and enemies lose you beyond 320 m. On lift-off, weapons stay cold for 4 seconds and shields charge from empty.

Miners and haulers carry guard drones that launch with you and defend your ship. They need a Drone controller fitted.

If a hull you bought is destroyed, you relaunch in a free starter hull. Hull insurance, sold at the Shipyard, covers 30 minutes and pays 70% toward a replacement.

Mining

Only mining hulls fit mining tools. Press R to target the nearest rock and see the ore it holds.

The mining laser is slow and safe. Point at the rock from a few hundred metres and hold F. The beam follows the rock's ring, a band around it, and cuts ore into your hold.

Blast charges are fast and risky. Fire one (F) onto a fault point, a weak spot the scan marks. Back off 120 m, press 5 to detonate, and fly into the chunks to scoop them.

A blast cracks the rock's ring, so the laser cannot cut that rock afterwards. 7 switches between the two tools.

Hauling and trade

The Market buys and sells ore, components and parts. Prices follow the station's real stock: a glut sells cheap and a shortage pays well.

Best runs lists the most profitable trades for your hold, from the prices you know. Cargo has weight, and a laden ship turns and accelerates worse.

Each station keeps a hangar for you. Goods you store there stay at that station.

Jobs, standing and heat

The Jobs board at a dock lists paid work from the powers and the freeports: Haul, Mine, Fight and Scout jobs, plus Depot missions. Whoever posts a job pays for it.

In flight, press I twice for Jobs here: the jobs in your system that suit your hull. Backspace drops the job you are on.

Standing is how far each power trusts you. Jobs for a power raise it, and attacking its ships or stations lowers it. Its bands are Hostile, Unwelcome, Neutral, Trusted and Partner.

You need Neutral or better to dock and trade with a power. At Hostile, its ships attack you on sight.

Heat is how hard a power's patrols are looking for you: Cool, Warm, Hot or Burning. Contraband (goods a power bans), theft and running from a customs scan raise it. It cools by itself.

A patrol that finds contraband gives you 15 seconds to bribe, surrender or run. The Black market page lists off-the-books work, which pays more the more heat it risks.

Contractor Rating runs from 1 to 10 and never falls. Work for the powers raises it, and a higher Rating unlocks bigger hulls and higher part grades.

Medium hulls and the Scout need Rating 2. Heavy Fighters, Bombers and the Advanced Scout need 4, Heavy Miners and Heavy Haulers 5, and the Heavy Bomber 6.

The dock

While you are docked, keys 1 to 5 pick the tabs.

Overview shows what you can do here now, a suggested next step, the state of the war and the news.

Trade holds the Market, Best runs and Fabricate, where you build components and parts for a labour fee.

Jobs holds the Jobs board and the Black market.

Ship holds Loadout (fit parts and rearm), Shipyard (hulls, repairs and insurance) and Hangar. Freeports add Refits, upgrades sold for credits.

Allegiance shows your Rating, standing and heat, your company and faction, and the powers and their leaders.

At a base you own, 6 opens Station: Works, Jobs and Company.

The Company screen (0) opens anywhere, docked or in flight. Its tabs are Profile, Allegiance, Holdings, Brand, Economy, Jobs and Black market.

The closed economy

Every good is made from ore mined out of real rock. Fresh rock drifts into worked-out fields every hour. The six ores are Ferrite, Cuprite, Iridium, Xenocrystal, Aurorite and Vantium.

Aurorite lies only in deep space. Vantium lies only in Kestrel Drift, The Shoal, Cinder Reach and Hollow Verge.

Ore is made into components, such as Alloy Plate, Conduit and Flux Coil. Components are made into parts: the weapons, tools and systems you fit to a hull.

Makers are the twelve corporations that build most parts: two at each power's home station and two at each freeport. Most makers' parts trade a strength for a weakness. Every station also stocks plain standard parts.

Parts come in grades 1 to 5, and a maker raises a line's grade through research. You can use grade 2 at Rating 2, grade 3 at Rating 4, grade 4 at Rating 6 and grade 5 at Rating 8.

Goods are never destroyed. A wreck spills salvage, and the rest becomes debris that re-forms into rock. Credits only move between pilots, the powers' treasuries and the Freeport Compact, which runs the freeports.

How bases work

A base is a station of your own. There are three kinds, in rising order: a Lair, a Depot and a Station.

Kits. Each base is built from a kit: a Lair Kit, a Depot Kit or a Station Kit. Every station's Market sells them, under Components.

Only a Heavy Hauler's hold takes a kit. Buy one in any other hull and it goes to your hangar at that station, where it waits for a Heavy Hauler.

O builds. In flight, O opens the build menu: build a Lair, build a Depot, or upgrade your Depot to a Station. Each line says what is missing.

Your company. Before you buy or use a Depot Kit or a Station Kit, set up your company on the Allegiance tab. You pick a name, logo, base design, colour and fees, and deposit 100,000 cr into its treasury.

The treasury pays your crews' wages, the jobs you post and your market's purchases. Fees and market sales flow into it. Move credits in or out on the Company screen's Profile tab.

Lair. A Lair is a hidden base beside a large asteroid, in a system with no station. It needs no company. You may keep four, one per system.

A Lair gives you a 200 t stash, repairs and rearming. Heat cools three times as fast while you are docked there. Stolen goods left in the stash lose their stolen mark after a minute or more.

A second level doubles the stash and adds a workshop and forge with no labour fee. A Lair stays hidden until a hostile power's scout comes within 5 km. After that, that power's ships attack it.

Depot. A Depot is an open base, shown on every map, with a market and a hangar for crews. Build one with a Depot Kit in a system with no station, outside the powers' home space. While you are docked at your Lair, a Depot Kit upgrades that Lair into a Depot.

A Depot does not hold its system. Its level, 1 to 3, comes from its development modules: level 2 at three module levels and level 3 at six.

Depot miners. A Depot mines only through its miners, never by itself. It comes with three, which fly to the system's rocks and bring ore home. A lost miner is rebuilt from the Depot's store, one every 4 minutes, while it holds Alloy Plate and Conduit.

Station. A Station is always upgraded from a Depot, never built fresh. Fly to your Depot with a Station Kit in your hold and press O.

A Station holds its system for you, and its hull and shield are stronger. It keeps the Depot's miners, modules and crews.

Buildings and modules. A Station has two separate things to build, both on its Works page. Its five buildings set its level, up to 5. Choose from Shipyard, External storage, Defence platform, Hangar, Forge and Market wing.

Development modules are different: storage, production, defence, armour and fighter hangars, for a Depot or a Station. They never raise a Station's level. A base holds two until you build Life support, which makes room for more.

A Shipyard building lays down capital ships: an Escort at Station level 3, a Destroyer at level 4 and a Carrier at level 5. Fly up to your capital ship and press E to board it.

Crews are AI pilots you hire on the Station tab's Jobs page. Hiring one costs its hull's price, at least 800 cr. Each then draws 300 cr an hour in wages and one Alloy Plate an hour from the base's store.

Set each crew to mine, haul, guard or stand idle. An unpaid crew leaves, and without Alloy Plate its ship is laid up. A base holds four crews; hangar modules and the Hangar building add room.

Jobs you post. On the same page, Post a job here offers Supply, Haul, Mine or Guard work to other pilots. Your bid (Low, Medium or Fast) sets how soon someone takes it. The treasury pays when the job is done.

Fees and market. Each base charges a transaction fee on trades, a services fee for repairs and rearming, and a docking fee. Set them in Company → Holdings; higher fees mean fewer visitors. Set what the base buys and sells in Company → Economy.

Access. On the Works page, under Who can run this base, you can let another pilot run it. They can hire and direct crews, post jobs and set fees, all from your treasury. They cannot open your Company page.

Your brand is your own line of parts, shaped on Company → Brand. Each strength costs a point and each weakness pays one back, so every part nets to zero. Build branded parts at your own base, under Fabricate → Your brand.

R&D (research) raises one brand line by one grade at a time, and only one project runs at once. The treasury pays 750, 2,000, 4,500 and 9,000 cr for grades 2 to 5.

The base caps the grade it can build. A Depot builds to grade 2, or grade 3 at Depot level 3. A Station builds to grade 4, or grade 5 with three buildings.

Raids. The powers, the Brood and the Unbound can send strikes at your bases. A base's Overview tab and Company → Holdings warn you when one is coming.

A power that breaks your Depot or Station either captures or destroys it. Break a captured base's hull yourself and it is yours again.

The powers, the Brood and the Unbound

Three powers fight for the galaxy: the Halcyon Accord, the Varkesh Dominion and the Ostrova Collective. Each has its own leader, hull designs and perk. The Allegiance tab shows them, and how the powers stand with each other.

The freeports are Mirrorwater, Coldwater Drift and Ember Wake. They are neutral, and they are never destroyed.

The Brood is an alien swarm that grows out of The Bloom, at the centre of the map. It is always hostile, and it grows stronger with every system it holds. Its Overgrowth seals jump gates to everyone but the Brood.

When the Brood holds ten systems, the powers sign the Containment Accord. They stop fighting each other and turn on the Brood until it holds fewer than eight.

The Unbound are pirates, led by the Dread Pirate. They are always hostile. Their jammers knock a ship dark so they can hook cargo from its hold.

Their base is Scrapheart Haven in The Wrack. You can dock there unless you attacked the Unbound in the last minute. Its market buys anything, stolen goods and contraband included.

The war and winning

A side wins by holding 24 systems for 30 minutes, and no win stands before hour 6. That is the Standard length, about 8 hours. The Long length, chosen on the start screen when a galaxy is made, asks for 24 systems held for an hour, with no win before hour 10. There is no deadline: a galaxy runs until someone wins. A galaxy saved before the length setting keeps the old rule, 20 systems for 10 minutes.

The galaxy has 46 systems, and 42 can be held. The three freeport systems and The Wrack cannot.

A system belongs to the side whose station, depot or outpost stands there. Your own Depots do not count. Your Stations do, once you declare a faction.

Outposts, such as the three Forges, can be claimed for a power. Buy that power's Control Marker on the Market, under Utility, and fit it to a fighter or bomber.

Then hold inside the ring of an outpost that is unclaimed or breached (shot down to zero hull). The claim uses up the marker, and the power pays you.

Declaring a faction. Once you own a Station, you can declare your own faction on the Allegiance tab. Your Stations then count toward the systems needed to win, and you can win the galaxy. Lose your last Station and the faction falls back into a company.

A power's leader may then propose an alliance or demand tribute. Answer on the Allegiance tab.

Legend points are kept between galaxies on this device. You earn them for milestones such as a Lair, a Depot, a Station, declaring a faction and winning. Legend levels unlock perks for your next start.

Playing with friends

Up to four pilots share one galaxy. On the title screen, one of you creates a galaxy for friends and sends the others the invite link or code.

The first pilot in hosts the galaxy. If the host leaves, another pilot takes over, and while nobody is online the galaxy waits. Press Enter in flight to chat.

Each pilot runs their own company, with its own treasury. To share a base, give a friend access on its Works page.
